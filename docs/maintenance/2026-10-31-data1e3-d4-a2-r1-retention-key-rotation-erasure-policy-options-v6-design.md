## R1-A — evidence classes and retention scopes (candidate invariants)

Treat these **as separate objects and purposes**, never an undifferentiated “audit log”:
1. **Native local audit event + N2-S sidecar mapping**: append-only local history; local trigger action is not a verified pilot/editor action; backfilled UID is `migration_backfill`, not original-time provenance.
2. **Imported original semantic event**: destination-account-scoped L1 unattached, original source user/audit/flight ID, original action/actor/time and old/new JSONB, canonicalization version/hash, exact source format/version.
3. **Every distinct signed snapshot observation**: immutable full signed-source digest + signer/source user, signature version/validation result at import, event UID/assignment kind, import operation and original source-event claim; C2's single *provisional logical display* never collapses observations.
4. **Source backup file / stored account backup**: separate, bounded original bytes, currently pruned by backup-center as documented. A full source archive may later be absent even when its import-time verification claim and observation remain.
5. **Minimal exception/erasure decision record**: separate access-restricted compliance record; exact fields and any personal-data content require legal necessity/minimization review. A digest, source-user ID or UUID can itself be personal/linkable data; do not label a persistent hash automatically anonymous.
6. **Signing-key metadata/material**: non-personal key ID, algorithm/version and activation/retirement metadata may be tracked; actual secret material only in an appropriate server-side secret store, never in DB audit rows, Git/docs/logs/backups or reviewer packets.

Retention must be **purpose, data-category, legal basis, event trigger and review/deletion schedule specific**; no invented fixed term for native/imported audit, observations, legal hold or key retention. The existing v2.8 privacy baseline preserves certain regulated evidence after account deletion but is *not* independent legal confirmation of a statutory retention period.

**Proof status stays granular:** `source_signature_verified_at_import` is a historical verification claim; `stored_event_hash_valid` is present local data integrity; `independently_recheck_source_membership` is **UNAVAILABLE** after the original source file is pruned unless an independently reviewed event-inclusion proof is retained. Never turn previously verified into presently reverified. If the original source key is compromised/revoked, keep the historical verification observation (with appropriate `COMPROMISED/UNKNOWN` caveat); do not silently rewrite as current “verified”.

## R1-K — candidate signing-key lifecycle (owner choice OPEN)

**K1 — Recommended: versioned verifier keyring with explicit new key IDs.**
- New future envelope/format **version** uses immutable `key_id` plus algorithm, purpose/domain, signer/source identity, digest, format version and canonicalization version in the authenticated material. Validate the entire version-specific envelope; do not trust unauthenticated claimed `key_id` or let it change payload membership semantics.
- Signing uses only a single **active** key ID; verification selects from a strictly configured allowlisted legacy-key inventory by authenticated format/algorithm/key ID. Historical v13 v1 envelopes have **no key ID**: a separately reviewed, bounded legacy verification strategy may attempt an approved list of known eligible v1 keys with identical v1 bytes/payload semantics; never blindly accept “any key” or quietly relabel old v13 signatures as v14.
- Rotation: provision and verify new key separately → exercise old-and-new signed golden test vectors and v13 originals → change *new signing* to new version/key → keep old verify-only secrets until approved disposition and dependent historical restore inventory can be handled. Old signing remains disabled. Missing/expired/disallowed key means **UNVERIFIABLE / BLOCK restore**, not “unsigned but acceptable”. A previously signed file is not re-signed to fabricate original source authorship.
- Compromise: disable compromised signing immediately, independently assess impacted archives and verification tiers; possible recoverability trade-off must be documented for owner/security decision. Do not imply key retirement restores authenticity retroactively. Never place key bytes or real signatures from pilot records in tests/docs.
- Key deletion timeline and hardware/provider storage requirements **NOT DECIDED**. Ensure a bounded key-retention rationale and reliable disaster-recovery of verification metadata without duplicating secrets in portable account backups.

**K2 — Not recommended: rotate one environment secret and replace all old signatures.** This would break source-digest/identity provenance, can invalidate v13 imports and could falsely attest historical origin. Not allowed absent a separate fundamentally different migration/security review.

**K3 — Pause v14 signature rollout until historical key inventories are known.** Safest fail-closed fallback if K1 cannot be supported without mixing `SESSION_SECRET` and `SIGNING_SECRET` historical meanings.

## R1-T — candidate retention/erasure policy choice (owner/legal OPEN)

**T1 — Recommended for design:** preserve immutable native/imported/observation evidence only while there is a documented lawful purpose and category-specific retention rule; keep backup archives under existing bounded pruning. At or after a legitimate erasure trigger, **do not treat immutability as a legal exemption**. Apply an exceptional, strongly authorized erasure route scoped to the individual/account and all occurrences, with an auditable decision outcome, not an ordinary ledger UPDATE/DELETE/grant. If no lawful basis for a particular retained record can be established, do not assert it may be kept indefinitely.

**T2 — Alternative:** indefinitely retain all signed events and all original archives “for audit”. **Rejected as default**: conflicts with minimization/storage-limitation principles and creates uncontrolled sensitive source-file duplication. No indefinite archival mandate has been established.

**T3 — Alternative:** delete all imported events immediately at ordinary account deletion without separate retained-signature/third-party evidence review. **Not safe as default**: can destroy legitimately retained signed evidence/other participant rights and leave opaque dangling C2 observations. Specific erasure scope must be reviewed and recorded instead.

**Law/legal review required:** GDPR Article 5 (minimisation/storage limitation), Article 17 (grounds, exceptions, and actual erasure), and Article 32 security; EDPB *2025 Coordinated Enforcement Action—Implementation of the right to erasure by controllers* adopted 10 Feb 2026 highlights retention periods, backups, inadequate anonymisation and legal-exception reasoning. These are authority sources for design analysis, NOT a finding that a particular FlyTally retention term or refusal is lawful. See https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj and https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-02/edpb_cef-report_2025_right-to-erasure_en.pdf .

## R1-E — extraordinary lawful erasure and re-import prevention (candidate flow, NO SQL)

0. **Legal assessment gate before data processing:** identify requester/controller, identity, data categories/parties, applicable jurisdiction, exact legal basis and retention exceptions/holds, deadlines, affected providers/backups and cross-product recipients. Legal hold must have specific authority, scope and expiry/review; a general “audit immutability” claim is insufficient. For unclear authority, do not execute irreversible deletion; escalate/review, record request timeline and meet obligations independently.
1. **Quiesce/reconcile** all active import/restore/export/signing jobs for affected account/identity with server + DB-authoritative controls. Exact concurrency and order with old pre_restore needs design; never rely on a UI-only lock.
2. **Inventory references** across native audit rows, native UID sidecars, imported logical event rows, *every* signed snapshot observation, provenance hashes, re-exported v14 evidence, v13 originals, stored/automatic/manual/pre_restore backups, deleted/voided/certified flight/FSTD, signatures, shares, other participants and provider-level disaster-recovery copies. Flag potential information about other people for separate rights assessment; do not automatically delete their independently owned evidence.
3. **Decision before mutation:** `RETAIN_WITH_DOCUMENTED_BASIS`, `ERASE_AUTHORIZED`, or `BLOCK_PENDING_REVIEW`. For `ERASE_AUTHORIZED`, determine which full rows/provenance/links must be removed or rendered non-identifying; pseudonymisation alone may remain linkable, and key destruction is not necessarily data erasure.
4. **Exceptional privileged transaction/saga**, separately approved and tested: no generic app privilege to delete ledgers; no FK CASCADE or old F9 broad DELETE; no dangling inconsistent event/observation/sidecar state. Exact schema/lock/operation ordering, affected backup invalidation and whether an intentionally minimal tombstone is lawful are OPEN. Failure before commit rolls back local changes; cross-provider deletion needs tracked independent completion and retries, not a false single-transaction claim.
5. **Revival guard:** a later stored/portable v13/v14 restore, pre_restore rollback, auto-backup recovery, copied archive or concurrency must **not reintroduce erased personal data**. A restricted suppression reference/deny mechanism may be necessary, but its stable identifiers/digests can be personal data and must have independently assessed lawfulness, minimisation and retention. If complete suppression is unavailable, block unsafe import rather than “restore successfully” and resurrect data.
6. **Outcome/proof:** audit who authorized process, legal reason, scope, execution status, affected stores, remaining justified holds, failure, deletion propagation, and any pending backups/provider actions using minimum necessary metadata. Never claim the original signed observations remain intact after authorized erasure; explicitly report evidence as `ERASED/UNAVAILABLE`, and suppress broken derived C2 logical claims. Do not represent a deletion as a pilot action or regulatory certification.

**Hard implementation blocker:** no routine append-only ledger may be introduced unless privacy deletion mapping, account deletion, backup pruning/provider propagation, exceptions and restoration resurrection are reviewed and tested. Existing v2.8 account-erasure path cannot simply ignore new tables.
